This research presents an APSIM-based modelling framework to explore the **plausible impacts of biological nitrification inhibition (BNI)** in cropping systems. It examines how BNI-related processes could affect nitrogen transformations, crop productivity and potential environmental outcomes under contrasting production environments.

The cropping systems assessed include sorghum in northern Australia, wheat in Western Australia and canola in South Australia. The work combines process-based crop modelling with scenario analysis to examine system responses and identify questions for further research.

The framework is a modelling contribution rather than a field validation of BNI effects. Simulated outcomes should therefore be interpreted in the context of model structure, assumptions and parameterisation, and should not be presented as measured field responses.

**Documented project roles:** team member on GRDC project CSP2406-021RTX, *Modelling plausible impacts of BNI*; and Output III co-lead on Proc-9177320, *Improving systems nitrogen-use efficiency and reducing losses through BNI*.
