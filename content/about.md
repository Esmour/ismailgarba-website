I am **Dr Ismail Ibrahim Garba**, an agricultural systems scientist and Research Scientist at CSIRO Agriculture and Food. My work integrates agronomy, process-based crop modelling and data analytics to investigate how crops and farming systems perform across environments, and how management can improve productivity, resource-use efficiency and environmental outcomes.

My research combines APSIM simulations with field experiments, climate and soil information, remote sensing and large-scale agricultural datasets. A central interest is understanding **genotype × environment × management (G × E × M) interactions** and using environmental characterisation (envirotyping) to explain variation in crop performance and inform management decisions.

At CSIRO, my research contributions span the National Risk Management Initiative (RiskWi$e), profitable yield frontiers and hyper-yielding crops, biological nitrification inhibition (BNI), nitrogen management and Northern Farming Systems. Alongside collaborative research, I **individually develop software applications and digital decision-support tools** that make modelling and analytical results accessible for research and practical decision-making.

## Education

- **PhD in Agronomy and Plant Science**, The University of Queensland, 2020–2023. Thesis: *Cover cropping in drylands for improved agronomic and environmental outcomes.*
- **MSc in Agronomy (Crops and Cropping Systems in Drylands)**, Bayero University Kano, 2016–2018. CGPA: 5.00/5.00.
- **Bachelor of Agriculture (Honours)**, Bayero University Kano, 2010–2015. First Class; CGPA: 4.81/5.00.

My doctoral research developed and validated a crop-mixture experimental methodology integrating field experimentation, remote sensing, statistics and APSIM. My earlier postgraduate research examined nutrient limitations and imbalances in maize-based systems in the Nigerian savannas, drawing on nutrient-diagnostic and crop-modelling approaches.
